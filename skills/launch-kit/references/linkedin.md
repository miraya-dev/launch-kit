# LinkedIn

## Limits and layout

- A post holds up to **3,000 characters**.
- Only the first two or three lines (roughly 200 characters, depending on the screen) show before "…see more". The opening must make people want to click.
- Short paragraphs of one to three lines, with blank lines between them. No walls of text.

## Shape

1. **Opening (fits before "…see more"):** a concrete, personal hook. A moment, a frustration, a result. Not "I'm thrilled to announce".
2. **The problem:** why this mattered to the maker.
3. **What they built:** one or two sentences, plain language.
4. **Two or three benefits:** short lines, outcomes not features.
5. **What they learned:** one honest lesson from building it. This is what makes LinkedIn posts worth reading.
6. **Link and question:** where to find it, plus a question that invites real comments.
7. **Hashtags:** 0 to 3 at the end.

## Practical notes

- Tag collaborators or companies only when they were involved.
- A link in the post is fine. Some creators put it in the first comment because external links may reduce reach; LinkedIn has not confirmed this.
- A short screen recording or a document (PDF carousel) of the demo often draws more attention than a link preview. The pack can include a brief for one.
- LinkedIn says it limits the reach of inauthentic activity such as **engagement pods** and automated comments. Never join a pod or use tools that comment or like automatically.

## In the pack

1. The opening alone in a `limit=210` block, so it fits before "…see more".
2. The full post in a `limit=3000` block.
3. A one-line brief for an optional image, video or document.
