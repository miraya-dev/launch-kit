# Hacker News: Show HN

Official rules (link both in the pack): [Show HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) · [Guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html) · [FAQ](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsfaq.html)

## What the rules say (paraphrased; read the originals)

- Show HN is for something people can **run or hold**: software they can try, hardware they can see. It should be easy to try, ideally without signups or emails.
- Not for: blog posts, newsletters, signup or landing pages, fundraisers, lists, or routine version bumps. A major overhaul can qualify.
- The maker must be **around to discuss it** in the comments.
- The title starts with `Show HN:`. The submit form limits titles to 80 characters. No ALL CAPS, exclamation marks or praise ("amazing", "the best").
- **Do not ask anyone to upvote or comment**, including friends and colleagues. Voting rings are detected and penalized.
- **No generated text.** The guidelines ask people not to post generated text in submissions, and not to post generated or AI-edited comments. So the pack gives notes, facts and likely questions; **the maker writes the final title, text and every reply in their own words.**
- Do not delete and resubmit. The FAQ allows a small number of reposts if a story got no significant attention.

## What works

- **Title:** `Show HN: <Name> – <what it does, plainly>`. Concrete beats clever. Write the notes for 3 options in the pack, each 80 characters or less.
- **URL:** the repository or a demo people can use immediately. Not a landing page with a waitlist.
- **Text** (many makers post it as the first comment right after submitting): who you are, what it is, why you built it, how it works technically, what is different from existing tools, what is not done yet, and one or two specific questions. Around 150 to 300 words.
- **Formatting:** HN renders almost no markdown. Blank lines separate paragraphs, `*text*` is italic, lines indented by two spaces are shown as code, URLs become links. No headings, bullets or `[text](url)` links.
- **Timing:** there is no magic hour. Pick a time when you can stay for 3 to 4 hours to answer comments; many makers choose a weekday morning, US Eastern time.

## In the comments

- Answer every question, kindly and briefly. Thank critics; admit limitations; say what you will change.
- Never argue about votes or ranking, and never comment from another account.
- If it does not take off, that is normal. Do not repost the next day; improve the project and come back with something new to show.

## In the pack

1. A ready check: easy to try without signup, README quick start works, maker free for 3 to 4 hours.
2. Three title options as `limit=80` blocks.
3. The URL to submit.
4. Notes for the text, as bullets the maker turns into their own words: who, what, why, how, what's different, what's missing, the question to ask.
5. A table of likely questions (why not X?, license?, how does it handle Y?, what's next?) with factual answers from the repo.
